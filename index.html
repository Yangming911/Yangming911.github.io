I am Shuqi (Suky) Wang, a PhD student in the Department of Automation, School of Electronic Information and Electrical Engineering, Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU), advised by Prof. Xiang Yin in the CPS group. I work on safety control theory for AI-embedded cyber-physical systems, with a particular focus on using conformal prediction as a safety interface between learning-based perception/decision modules and physical control loops. More >

Prior to my PhD, I earned my bachelor's degree in Artificial Intelligence (Robotics) Honored Class from SJTU. I have also worked at the National University of Singapore (NUS) AI4SG Lab with Prof. Yi-Chieh (EJ) Lee.

Research Interests:

  • Conformal Safety Interfaces. Wrapping black-box AI modules (VLMs, neural predictors, learned dynamics) with distribution-free, finite-sample guarantees, and propagating these guarantees through planning and control layers for end-to-end safety.
  • Uncertainty-Aware Learning-Based Control. Chance-constrained neural MPC, sampling-based control (MPPI) with information-theoretic guarantees, and formal diagnosability of discrete-event systems under statistical uncertainty.

If you are interested in research collaboration, feel free to contact me.

#Research Highlights

CoRL 2026

CoSMO: Open-World Safe Robot Navigation

Conformal semantic mapping turns vision-language embeddings into calibrated safety maps for open-world robot navigation, adapting to new hazards and distribution shifts with delayed expert feedback.

Paper Project Video
Conformal Prediction Robot Navigation Open World Safety Guaranteed VLM
NAHS 2026

Chance-Constrained Neural MPC under Uncontrollable Agents

A neural MPC framework with formal probabilistic safety guarantees against stochastic, state-coupled uncontrollable agents: region-wise robust conformal prediction constructs time-dependent uncertainty bounds under policy-induced distribution shift, and a two-loop sequential convex programming algorithm solves the resulting chance-constrained problem.

#About Me

In Isaac Asimov's Robots and Empire, the Spacers fell not because they used robots, but because they used robots to avoid becoming pioneers themselves. This story has stayed with me. I hope to contribute, even in small ways, to a future in which humans use robots not merely to preserve comfort, but to better understand robots, the world, and ourselves. Human–robot cooperation should help us move safely beyond our local optima, rather than trapping us inside them.
